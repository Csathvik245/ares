# ARES — the Jezero science case

Why ARES-1 walks at Jezero's delta front. For slides 1–2, the project page and the README. Sources are in [`sources.md`](sources.md).

## The paragraph

Jezero Crater once held a lake. A river cut through the crater wall and dropped its sediment where it met still water, building a fan-shaped **delta** that is still visible from orbit. Perseverance's cameras later confirmed that the fan was laid down in a lake, and showed boulders carried in by short, powerful floods (Mangold et al. 2021). Orbital spectra from CRISM see **clay minerals and carbonates** in the delta and around the crater (Goudge et al. 2015; Horgan et al. 2020). Both form with water, and both are good at trapping and preserving organic material, so fine lake and delta sediments are among the best places on Mars to look for **signs of past life**. Perseverance has already cored rocks here that were altered by water and contain carbonate (Farley & Stack 2022, 2023). ARES-1's stops follow that story: the Séítah and Máaz rocks of the crater floor, which the rover sampled, and a new target at the delta front chosen from the CRISM clay signal. A sign of life found in rocks like these would only be a *possible* biosignature: the same chemistry can form without life, which is why samples need careful study.

## Short version (one line)

Jezero was a lake with a river delta. Its clays and carbonates formed in water and can preserve signs of past life, so it is where Perseverance went, and where ARES-1 walks.

## Wording to avoid

- **No age number in the main text.** We have not opened a NASA page or paper that gives the lake's age. Press summaries of Mangold et al. 2021 say about 3.6–3.8 billion years (Late Noachian to Early Hesperian). The pitch line "3.5 billion years ago" in `CLAUDE.md` is **unverified**: use "more than 3.5 billion years ago" only after someone opens a NASA source that says it, and add it to `sources.md`.
- Don't say "Jezero has life" or "carbonates prove life". Say "may preserve signs of past life".
- Don't say Perseverance visited our delta-front stop. It did not; it is our own target from the CRISM clay signal.
- Our CRISM layer is a relative index from one orbital scene, not a mineral abundance (`assumptions.md`).
